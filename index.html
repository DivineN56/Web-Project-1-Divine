General History


Who Invented Basketball

Basketball was invented by a Canadian-American physical educator by the name of DR. James Naismith in december of 1891 at the international YMCA training school (now SpringField College) in springfield massachusetts. During the Winter of 1891 his students needed a safe indoor game to keep them fit and reduce risks of collision to reduce injury.


The Original Thirteen Rules

  • The ball may be thrown in any direction with one or both hands

  • The ball may be batted in any direction with one or both hands (never the fist)

  • The player may not run with the ball. The player must throw from the spot on which he catches it. Exception for someone who caught while running at good speed if he tries to stop

  • The ball must be held in or between the hands, The arms or body must not be used for holding it

  • No shouldering, holding, pushing, tripping, or striking in any way, The first infringement of this rule by any player shall count as foul, The second shall disqualify him until the next goal is made, Or if there was evident intent to injure the person they are out for the whole game no substitute allowed

  • A foul is striking at the ball with your fist
  • If either side makes three consecutive fouls, It shall count as a point for the opponent (Consecutive means without the opponenet making a foul in the mean time)

  • A goal shall be made when the ball is thrown or batted from the grounds into the basket and stays there, providing those defending the goal do not touch or disturb the goal. If the ball rests on the edges, and the opponent moves the basket, it shall count as a goal.

  • When the ball goes out of bounds, it shall be thrown into the field of play by the person first touching it. In case of a dispute, the umpire shall throw it straight into the field. The thrower-in is allowed five seconds; if he holds it longer, it shall go to the opponent. If any side persists in delaying the game, the umpire shall call a foul on that side.

  • The umpire shall be judge of the men and shall note the fouls and notify the referee when three consecutive fouls have been made. He shall have power to disqualify men according to Rule 5.

  • The referee shall be judge of the ball and shall decide when the ball is in play, in bounds, to which side it belongs, and shall keep the time. He shall decide when a goal has been made, and keep account of the goals with any other duties that are usually performed by a referee.

  • The time shall be two 15-minute halves, with five minutes' rest between.

  • The side making the most goals in that time shall be declared the winner. In case of a draw, the game may, by agreement of the captains, be continued until another goal is made.

How the NBA came to be

The NBA all starts with the midwest basketball conference which was formed in the summer of 1935 as an industrially based proffessional basketball league. It was organized by Frank Kautsky (owner of the indianapolis Kautskyd) and Paul Sheeks (recreational director for th firestone tire company). DR. H.C Carlson, the basketball coach at the university of pittsburgh, Served as the league commissioner. The league was made up of coporate-sponsored industrial teams such as Akron firestone and Indianapolis U.S tires and independent professional clubs, Teams informally arranged their own regular season schedule.

Championship Results

  • Eastern division champion: Akron Firestone
  • Western division champion: Indianapolis Kautskys
  • Overall championship winner: Chicago duffy florals

Transition to NBL in 1937

On october 6, 1937, the midwest basketball conference officially changes its name to the National Basketball League (NBL). This rebranding transformed the league from a loosely organized, semi professional group into a formalized, fully professional basketball league. Before it became the NBA the NBL consisted of 10 teams

  • Anderson duffy packers
  • Syracuse nationals
  • Tri-cities blackhawks
  • Sheboygan Red skins
  • Waterloo hawks
  • Denver nuggets
  • Hammond calumet buccaneers
  • Oshkosh-allstars
  • Detroit vagabond kings
  • Dayton Rens

The BAA

The basketball association of america (BAA) was a professional north-amerian basketball league founded on june 6th 1946, by major ice hockey arena owners who wanted to fill their venues on nights when their hockey teams were travelling or not playing. The first game was played on november 1, 1946, between the new york knickerbockers and the toronto huskies at maple leaf garden. Unlike the midwest basketball conference that played in gymnasium, The BAA played in major city arenas like Boston garden and Madison square garden

Champions

  • 1947: Philadelphia warriors
  • 1948: Baltimore Bullets
  • 1949: Minneapolis lakers

The Merger

The historic merger between the BAA and the NBL took place on august 3, 1949, making the national basketball association (NBA). Out of 10 NBL teams only 7 joined the NBA and 10 BAA teams, making the NBA a total of 17 teams at first which were

  • Boston celtics
  • New york knicks
  • Philadelphia warriors (now golden state warriors)
  • Baltimore bullets
  • Wasshington capitols
  • Syracuse nationals (now Philadelphia 76ers)
  • Minneapolis lakers (now los angeles lakers)
  • Rochester royals (now sacramento kings)
  • Fortwayne pistons (now the deroit pistons)
  • Chicago stags
  • St.louis bombers
  • Tri-cities blackhawks (now the atlanta hawks)
  • Anderson packers
  • Denver nuggets (completely different from the denver nuggets of today)
  • Sheboygan redskins
  • Waterloo hawks
  • Indianapolis oympians

The massive layout was unsustainable, small town gymnasiums couldn't generate the revenue needed to travel across the country to play big city arena teams. Within a few years the league aggresively contracted, shrinking to its smallest size of just 8 surviving franchises by 1954. The modern NBA chose to keep the BAA's governing body and chose the BAA's history as their official history. To this day the NBA still treats the NBL teams as an expansion, completely erasing prior NBL records and statistics.


The ABA-NBA Merger

The american basketball association (ABA) was a professional mens basketball league that operated in direct competition with the NBA from 1967 to 1976. It was famous for its red, white and blue basketball which stood out against the leather basketball used by the NBA. The ABA also invented the 3-point shot. It featured iconic superstars like Julius Erving and George Gervin. The NBA and ABA merged on june 17 1976 ending a nine year rivalry. The agreement was officially structured as an expansion.
